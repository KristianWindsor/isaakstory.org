# Evangelical-Augsburg Parish in Pabianice

The Evangelical-Augsburg Parish in Pabianice is a parish of the Evangelical Church of the Augsburg Confession in Poland, dedicated to Saints Peter and Paul and belonging to the Warsaw Diocese. In 2018, the parish had approximately 100 members.[1]

## History

In the early 19th century, alongside rapid industrial growth, the number of Protestant immigrants from Germany settling in Pabianice began to rise. From 1804, Evangelical services were conducted at the Kraków Chapter Castle by Pastor Friedrich Wilhelm Schramm, who traveled from Piotrków. The Evangelical-Augsburg parish in Pabianice was established in 1818; however, despite the completion of administrative formalities, it did not become operational. It was not until 1827 that the parish was re-established and a decision was made to build a parish church. At its inception, the parish served 320 families from Pabianice and surrounding localities (including Markówki, Starowa Góra, and Bukowiec). The Church of Saints Peter and Paul was erected between 1827 and 1832, with subsequent decades seeing its expansion and furnishing. Concurrently, an Evangelical cemetery was established adjacent to the existing Catholic one. In the 1870s, a public garden was created on parish-owned land along Zamkowa Street. By 1904, the Pabianice congregation numbered approximately 12,400 believers, making it one of the largest in the country. Morning and evening services began to be held, including services in Polish. Bible study sessions, children's services, and missionary gatherings and observances were introduced. In addition to pastoral care, the parish engaged in educational activities. At the turn of the 20th century, it operated ten elementary schools and one *kantorat* (parish school), educating a total of 1,100 children. An Evangelical nursery for preschool-aged children was also established. A home for the elderly was opened in 1900. Also active were the Evangelical-Augsburg Society for the Care of Girls (*Jungfrauenverein*) and the Evangelical-Augsburg Society of Young Men (*Jünglingsverein*), as well as the Association of Evangelical Women (*Frauenverein*), whose primary mission was caring for the poor and the sick.

The flourishing of the Protestant community in Pabianice was interrupted by World War II. In 1943, the pastor was sent to the front due to his lack of submission to the Nazis and a conflict with the local Gestapo. In the post-war years, the parish lacked a resident pastor. Mieczysław Moczar issued a ban on holding Evangelical services. During the Polish People's Republic era, pastoral care was provided by clergy commuting from Zduńska Wola. The parish finally received a resident pastor again in 1973.

## Współczesna działalność

Currently, services are held every Sunday at the parish church in Pabianice, located at 8 Zamkowa Street. On the last Sunday of each month, concerts from the "Musical Sunday" series take place under the artistic direction of Magdalena Hudzieczek-Cieślar. Every October, in collaboration with the City Hall, the parish organizes the "World Music in Pabianice" International Festival[2], hosting instrumentalists and vocalists from France, the Netherlands, Great Britain, Korea, Slovakia, Russia, Ukraine, Georgia, Greece, and Poland. The parish also has a choir that performs at diocesan choir gatherings and ecumenical events.

Next to the church stands the pastors' residence (built in the Neo-Gothic style between 1900 and 1902), which still houses the parish office and the priest's living quarters.
