*From the Kirchliches Monatsblatt der Synode Cammin (Church Monthly of the Cammin Synod) 4–7/1910, by Pastor Strecker, Fritzow*

### Wedding Customs in the Parish of Fritzow around 1750

The "Monatsblätter für Pommersche Geschichte und Altertumskunde" (Monthly Papers for Pomeranian History and Antiquities) already reported on this in the year 1906 — a paper that deserves to be read in wider circles. It is published by the Gesellschaft für Pommersche Geschichte (Society for Pomeranian History) in Stettin. One can become a member of this society through an annual contribution of 6 Marks, for which one receives the "Monatsblatt" and other publications. But because the paper is accessible to only a few, that report may find a place in our own Monatsblatt as well. After all, one does like to hear how the old folk went about things.

### The Choice of Partner

In the choosing of a bride or a bridegroom, particular affection was seldom what mattered; usually it was the will of the parents, or — if these were no longer living — of the nearest relatives. In any case, these relatives regarded it as a great want of respect if they were not consulted in the choice. And though the mutual inclination of the betrothed pair was given little heed, quarreling and incompatibility in marriages were nonetheless rare.

If a widower or a widow wished to marry again, the grown children too had a decisive voice — all the more so because a considerable Altenteil (retirement provision) was set aside for the old folk when the farm was handed over. Certain years were also often fixed, determining how long the parents should go on managing the farm before they signed it over to the son or daughter.

### Arranged Marriages and Double Weddings

Once the parents had resolved that the children should marry, the bride or bridegroom was usually chosen from among the members of the "Freundschaft" (the kin, the extended family circle) who were known to be good managers and who also brought along a respectable quantity of beds, linen, horses, and cows. If an opportunity arose to arrange a double wedding, it was seized with particular eagerness. The Fritzow marriage registers bear witness to this. If, for example, there were two sons in one family and two daughters in another, then these had to marry one another. The eldest son remained on the father's farm; the other went to his parents-in-law.

Sometimes it happened that widowed persons married on the condition that their own children — brought into the marriage and still young — should later be joined together and possess both farms; or, if one couple was already grown, should straightaway take over the vacated farm.

### The Dowry Negotiations

Over the dowry (among the well-to-do, besides beds, linen goods, and household gear, also 4 horses, 4 cows, and a few hundred Taler) there was much haggling. One party gave up some of its conditions, the other added to its own, so that it was really a bargaining much like the purchase of a horse. If the parties could not agree, they parted calmly, and nothing came of the whole fine plan. But if agreement was reached, it was at once arranged how and when the wedding should be held. To many a tender-feeling soul such bargaining may seem repellent; at any rate, it forestalls many disappointments and disputes later on.

### The Hochzeitsbitter (Wedding Inviters)

The wedding festivities began with the Hochzeitsbitter (wedding inviters), two farmhands who rode from house to house on horseback, decked out with long, colorful ribbons streaming down from shoulder and hat, and with artificial bouquets of flowers as well. They rode right into each house and came to a halt on the great Diele (the threshing-floor hall), or indeed only in the parlor itself, to recite their invitation verses. These verses were partly rhymed; they told of the institution of the married estate and requested those invited to appear at the wedding house, also to help out with necessary utensils, to lodge guests, and so forth.

### The Hochzeitslied (Wedding Song)

This invitation was called the Hochzeitslied (wedding song), and it ran as follows. *(In this section and the next, the German original stands in the blockquote and the English translation follows beneath it.)*

**Stanza 1:**

> Nun ihr lieben Schwäger und Freunde insgemein und alle, die hier beisammen sein, ich wollt euch durch meine Bitte und Gaben freundlich ersucht und gebeten haben, daß ihr möget von der Güte sein, eine Minute oder zehn stille zu sein oder das sechste Viertel von einer Stund, anzuhören die Rede aus meinem Mund. Denn ich bin anjetzo schon allhier doch man sehr schlecht von Zier.

Now, you dear brothers-in-law and friends all together, and all who are gathered here — through my request and my gifts I would have kindly asked and begged of you that you might be so good as to be still a minute, or ten, or the sixth quarter of an hour, to hear the speech from my mouth. For I am already here now, though very poorly adorned.

**Stanza 2:**

> Ich werde grüßen von meinem Hauswirt und meiner Hauswirtin, nicht allein von denen sondern auch von Braut und Bräutigam, als nämlich von dem ehr- und wohlgeachteten Bräutigam N. N., nicht allein von dem sondern auch von seiner ehr- und tugendsamen Jungfer Braut N. N.

I bring greetings from my Hauswirt (master of the house) and my Hauswirtin (mistress of the house), and not only from them but also from the bride and the bridegroom — namely from the honorable and highly esteemed bridegroom N. N., and not only from him but also from his honorable and virtuous maiden bride N. N.

> Als euch ohne Zweifel wohl bewußt sein wird, daß sich diese beiden Personen ehelich verlobt und versprochen haben und haben ein eheliches Verlöbnis gehalten und sind in der christlichen Kirche durch priesterliche Kopulation dreimal aufgeboten und gedenken sich nach Gottes Ordnung und Schickung in den Stand der heiligen Ehe zu begeben, ist derhalben ihr und mein ganz dienstfreundliches Bitten, daß der Hauswirt mit seiner lieben Hausfrau, Kinder und Gesinde mögen am zukünftigen Freitag, solange die Hochzeit werden wird, ihre angenehmen Gäste sein und halten Hochzeit mit uns und nehmen vorlieb am Essen und Trinken, was der liebe Gott durch seinen milden und reichen Segen bescheren wird. Das wollen sie ungeweigert von euch haben.

As it will doubtless be well known to you that these two persons have become betrothed and pledged to one another in marriage, and have held a marriage engagement, and have been proclaimed three times in the Christian church for priestly wedding (Kopulation, the church marriage rite), and intend, according to God's order and providence, to enter into the estate of holy matrimony — it is therefore their and my most humble and friendly request that the Hauswirt, with his dear wife, children, and household servants, may on the coming Friday, for as long as the wedding shall last, be their welcome guests and hold the wedding with us, and make do with the food and drink that dear God, through His mild and abundant blessing, shall provide. This they wish to have from you without refusal.

**Stanza 3:**

> Ferner lassen sie euch bitten, die Manns und die Gesellen, die Jungfern und die Jungfrauen (d. h. Frauen), daß sie sich am zukünftigen Freitag um 10 Uhr in ihre Behausung verfügen und erscheinen und ziehen mit Braut und Bräutigam über Feld nach der christlichen Kirche vor der Trau und helfen ihren Ehestand zieren und vermehren, mit einem andächtigen Gebet beiwohnen, um eine glückliche und wohlgeratene Ehe den lieben Gott helfen anrufen; nach geschehener Trau wieder umkehren ins Hochzeitshaus und vorlieb nehmen allda billig, auswärtig, großgünstig, gutwärtig vorlieb auf- und annehmen.

Further, they bid you — the men and the young fellows, the maidens and the Jungfrauen (that is, the married women) — to betake yourselves to their dwelling on the coming Friday at 10 o'clock and to appear, and to travel with bride and bridegroom across the field to the Christian church before the marriage rite, and to help adorn and increase their married estate, to attend it with a devout prayer, to help call upon dear God for a happy and well-blessed marriage; and after the marriage rite is done, to turn back again to the wedding house and there to make do, fittingly and generously, to accept and receive with good will.

> Von der Mahlzeit zum Trunk Fröhlich zum Sprung, mit Tanzen und Springen und andere gute Kurzweil helfen die Hochzeit zu Ende bringen.

From the meal to the drink, merrily to the leap, with dancing and springing and other good sport, help bring the wedding to its close.

**Stanza 4:**

> Ferner lassen sie euch bitten, daß ihr euch möget zu rechter Zeit einstellen mit einem Wagen und 4 Pferden, mit einer Jungfer vier oder fünf, mit einem Junggesellen sechs, sieben oder acht, soviel des Herren Haus vermag.

Further, they bid you to present yourselves in good time with a wagon and 4 horses — with a maiden, four or five; with a bachelor, six, seven, or eight — as many as the master's household can manage.

**Stanza 5:**

> Ferner lassen sie euch bitten, daß ihr möget keine notwendige Sache vorwenden, damit die Tage der Ehe nicht geschwächet sondern vielmehr gestärket werden. Werdet ihr wieder einen Sohn oder eine Tochter ausgeben oder eine andere Elation (d. h. Kollation — Ausrichtung) anstellen, sofern sie dazu geladen werden, wollen sie auch wiederum Hülf und Beistand tun.

Further, they bid you not to plead any pressing matter as an excuse, so that the days of the marriage may not be weakened but rather strengthened. And should you in turn give away a son or a daughter, or arrange some other Elation (that is, Kollation — the holding of a feast), insofar as they are invited to it, they too will again render help and assistance.

**Stanza 6:**

> Ferner lassen sie euch bitten, daß ihr möget Braut und Bräutigam verschmaden (verschmähen) nicht, mir ausgesandter Bote daneben auch nicht; denn das habe ich getan Braut und Bräutigam zu Ehren. Ich wollte wünschen, daß ich meine Rede könnte weiter vermehren. Was wir werden sehen am zukünftigen Freitag um Klock zehn oder elfen, da wolle uns der liebe Gott auch zu verhelfen.

Further, they bid you not to scorn (verschmähen) bride and bridegroom, nor me, the messenger sent out, either; for I have done this in honor of bride and bridegroom. I could wish that I were able to make my speech go further still. What we shall see on the coming Friday at ten o'clock (Klock — the clock) or eleven — to that may dear God also help us.

**Stanza 7:**

> Ferner bitte ich für meine Person: habe ich nicht gut gebeten, so möget ihr es desto besser verstehen, desto eher kommen, desto länger bleiben, desto lustiger und fröhlicher sein. Denn ich bin noch jung an Jahren, ich habe noch wenig erfahren, jung in Ehren. Wenn ich werd älter werden, hoffe ich dieses noch alles besser zu lernen; denn ich bin in der Verhoffung, ihr werdet euch zu rechter Zeit einstellen.

Further, I ask for my own part: if I have not invited you well, then may you understand it all the better, come all the sooner, stay all the longer, and be all the merrier and gladder. For I am still young in years, I have yet experienced little, young and honorable. When I grow older, I hope to learn all this still better; for I am in the hope that you will present yourselves in good time.

**Stanza 8:**

> Gott hat den Ehestand eingesetzt; das war der Erst und bleibt der Letzt. Von allen andern in der Welt ihm diese Heirat wohlgefällt.

God set the married estate in place; it was the first and remains the last. Of all the others in the world, this marriage is well pleasing to Him.

**Stanza 9:**

> Gern möchte ich gesehen Han, wie Adam und Eva haben getan, wie sie beide ihr Herz erquickt, als sie sich haben einander erblickt.

Gladly would I have looked on to see what Adam and Eve had done, how both of them refreshed the heart when they caught sight of one another.

**Stanza 10:**

> Wie wird doch Adam haben gelacht, als er vom Schlaf ist aufgewacht. Er legt sich nieder ganz allein, stand auf und fand ein Jungferlein.  
> Adam bedachte sich nicht weit, er zog nicht lange auf die Freit; er nahm die erste, die er fand und begab sich mit ihr in den Ehestand.  
> Er traf es auch sehr recht und wohl und tat damit, was er tun soll, daß er die erste bald behält; Gott hat sie ihm selbst dargestellt.  
> Wie wird sich Eva haben gestellt, die Fürstin und Kaiserin der Welt, weil sie zur Ehe kam, bald da sie fing zu leben an.

How Adam must have laughed, when he awoke from sleep. He laid himself down all alone, stood up, and found a little maiden.  
Adam did not deliberate far, he did not go a-wooing long; he took the first one he found and entered the married estate with her.  
He judged it, too, quite right and well, and did thereby what he ought — that he keep the first one straightaway; God Himself had presented her to him.  
How Eve must have carried herself, the princess and empress of the world, because she came to be married, so soon after she began to live.

**Stanza 11:**

> Er gab ihr auch Hülf mit ihr der ganzen Welt samt ihrer Zier. Daher war Adam gar sehr reich, daß ihm noch keiner ist worden gleich.

He gave her too, and with her the whole world together with all its adornment, as a help. And so was Adam very rich indeed, so that none has yet become his equal.

**Stanza 12:**

> Eva auch kein Bedünknis trägt, ihm diese Verbeugnis (nicht?) abschlägt. Vor allen andern in der Welt ihr unser Adam wohlgefällt.  
> Ein Wunderding allhier geschieht, desgleichen wir erfahren nicht: Adam war lang noch nicht ein Jahr, da er zum Ehstand tüchtig war.

Eve, too, bears no misgiving, does not (?) refuse him this homage. Before all others in the world, our Adam is well pleasing to her.  
A wondrous thing happens here, the like of which we do not learn: Adam was not yet a year old when he was fit for the married estate.

**Stanza 13:**

> Eva war auch keine Stund nicht alt, taugt doch zum Weibe dem Adam bald. Doch wo hat sie gelernet wohl, wie sie ihre Wirtschaft führen soll?  
> Sie hat ja keine Mutter nicht, die sie hierin hat abgericht. Gott, der sie Adam zugewandt, der hat ihr solches eingepflanzt.

Eve, too, was not one hour old, yet was soon fit to be Adam's wife. But where, then, did she learn well how she should run her household?  
She had no mother, after all, to train her up in this. God, who turned her toward Adam, He planted such things within her.

**Stanza 14:**

> Wo ist ein Tischler hergebracht, der ihr das Brautbett hat gemacht so fein und sauber schön geziert, wie diesen jungen Eheleuten gebührt?  
> Das tat der weise Zimmermann, der künstliche Arbeit machen kann. Gott, der die ganze Welt erbaut, dem war solch Brautbett anvertraut.

Where is a joiner to be found who made her the bridal bed so fine and clean and beautifully adorned, as befits these young married folk?  
That the wise Carpenter did, who can do artful work. God, who built the whole world, to Him was such a bridal bed entrusted.

**Stanza 15:**

> Welcher Priester hat doch die Gnad, der sie gekopulieret hat? Das hat der Herr Christus selber getan; der war Priester und auch Freiermann.

And which priest has the grace, the one who married them? That the Lord Christ Himself did; He was priest and matchmaker both.

**Stanza 16:**

> Was mögen das wohl insgemein für Hochzeitsgäst gewesen sein, die sie allda gewacht froh? Das tat die engelische Gelorio.

And what, all together, might these wedding guests have been, who kept glad watch over them there? That the angelic Gloria did.

**Stanza 17:**

> Wer ist im Ehstand doch so rein, als diese zwei gewesen sein? Eva dem Adam treu verbleibt, Adam liebt auch kein ander Weib.

Who in the married estate is so pure as these two ever were? Eve remains faithful to Adam, and Adam loves no other wife.

**Stanza 18:**

> Darum bespiegelt euch daran, ihr Junggesellen, Frau und Mann. Beileibe nicht nach andern schaut! Ein jeder lieb, was ihm vertraut.

So take a mirror of yourselves in this, you bachelors, wife and man. By no means look after others! Let each love the one entrusted to him.

**Stanza 19:**

> Nun, ihr Lieben, seid nicht höhnisch, Gott will's haben schlecht und recht; darum sind wir seine erkauften Knecht. Darum wollen wir leben, wie uns die Schrift lehrt, daß zu allen Zeiten von uns Gott (werde) gefürchtet und geehrt.

Now, dear ones, do not be scornful; God will have it plain and honest; therefore we are His bought-and-paid-for servants. Therefore let us live as the Scripture teaches us, that at all times God may be feared and honored by us.

> Der Ehstand, den Gott zuerst im Paradiese eingesetzet hat und auch zugleich begäbet mit seiner Gnad, der soll (sein) in aller Zucht und Ehrbarkeit, wie ihn Gott geordnet hat zu jeder Zeit.

The married estate, which God first set in place in Paradise and at the same time endowed with His grace — that estate shall be lived in all discipline and honor, as God has ordered it for every age.

**Stanza 20:**

> Darum, ihr Lieben, so lasset uns nicht Übels tun, daß diese Hochzeit nicht werde eine gewisse Unruh; sondern lasset uns gute Christen sein, essen und trinken und fröhlich sein, nicht fressen und saufen wie die Ungeheuer.  
> Wird oft beklagt im ewigen Feuer! Daß nicht geliebet der reiche Mann, der durft auch nicht darüber geklaget Han, da er nun sitzet in der Höllen Glut; solches hat ihm verdienet sein eigen Fleisch und Blut.

Therefore, dear ones, let us do no evil, that this wedding may not turn into some disturbance; but let us be good Christians, eat and drink and be merry, not gorge and guzzle like the monsters.  
It is often lamented in the eternal fire! That the rich man loved not is why he had no right to complain of it either, now that he sits in the glow of hell; this his own flesh and blood earned for him.

**Stanza 21:**

> So laßt uns nun auch Josefs Zucht vorstellen wohl, und auch Tobiam, da er Hochzeit machen wollt. Da haben sie vor allem gebeten sehr, sodaß es Gott habe im Himmel erhört, und der böse Geist vertrieben ward, daß (er) ihm nicht schadete am Vorhaben seiner Hochzeit, und der junge Tobias am Leben bleib, nicht wie er den sieben vorigen hat getan.

So let us now also set before us Joseph's chastity, and Tobias too, when he would hold his wedding. Then above all they prayed most earnestly, so that God in heaven heard it, and the evil spirit was driven off, so that it did him no harm in the undertaking of his wedding, and young Tobias stayed alive — not as it had done to the seven before him.

**Stanza 22:**

> Diese Bitte tu ich aus Liebe zu euch; ihr Lieben, seid nicht eigen gleich, daß ihr könnt tun, wie es euch gefällig ist, und ihr es euch gerne gönnet zu jeder Frist.

This request I make out of love for you; dear ones, do not be so set in your own ways, that you do only as it pleases you and gladly indulge yourselves at every turn.

**Stanza 23:**

> Darum wollen wir christlichem Gebrauch nach leben und diesen geratenen Brautleuten eine ehrliche Hochzeit halten; denn ein Bräutigam, wenn er gedenket zu frein, so nimmt er sich dreierlei Gedanken ein: Zum ersten gedenket er an die Schönheit, zum andern an den Reichtum, zum dritten an die Tugend.

Therefore let us live according to Christian custom and hold an honorable wedding for this well-favored couple; for a bridegroom, when he thinks of wooing, takes up three kinds of thought: first he thinks of beauty, second of wealth, third of virtue.

> I. Zum Ersten was ist die Schönheit? Die Schönheit ist nichts anderes als die Blume, die im Garten stehet; und so ein Bräutigam kommt und nimmt eine Tochter ihrer Mutter, und sie viel Leidenszeit bekommt, so vergeht ihre Schönheit.

I. First, what is beauty? Beauty is nothing other than the flower that stands in the garden; and when a bridegroom comes and takes a daughter from her mother, and she comes to have much time of sorrow, then her beauty passes away.

> II. Zum andern der Reichtum. Denn gedenket mancher: möchtest du diese Jungfer Braut bekommen! Die hat viel Geld und Güter, die könnte dir helfen die Tage deines Lebens! Aber was ist der Reichtum, wenn der liebe Gott nicht seinen Segen dazu gibt!

II. Second, wealth. For many a man thinks: if only you could get this maiden bride! She has much money and goods, she could help you through the days of your life! But what is wealth, if dear God does not add His blessing to it!

> III. Zum dritten die Tugend. Die Tugend achte ich für das Allerbest, denn ein tugendsam Weib kommt vom Herrn und wird dem gegeben, der Gott fürchtet. Darum freue dich deines Weibes ihrer Tugend, denn sie ist lieblich wie eine Rose usw. vergl. Sprüche Sal. 5.

III. Third, virtue. Virtue I hold to be the very best of all, for a virtuous wife comes from the Lord and is given to the man who fears God. Therefore rejoice in the virtue of your wife, for she is lovely as a rose, etc. — compare Proverbs of Solomon 5.

**Stanza 24:**

> Gott hat den Ehstand selbst geehrt und einem jeden das Seine bescheert. Ich werde schließen, meine Rede enden müssen, was mir befohlen ist, kurz verwenden. Ich will aufhören und euch über Gebühr (nicht?) aufhalten. Gott wird mit seiner Gnade über euch walten und uns mit seinem Geist regieren, daß wir mögen ein anständiges Leben führen. Da putzet die Pferde und schmieret die Schuh und ziehet mit mir nach der Hochzeit zu. —

God Himself has honored the married estate and granted to each his own. I will close, must bring my speech to an end, and briefly discharge what has been charged to me. I will stop and not (?) detain you beyond due measure. God will rule over you with His grace and govern us with His Spirit, that we may lead a decent life. Now groom the horses and grease the shoes and set out with me toward the wedding. —

**Stanza 25:**

> Meine Bitte ist zu bedenken: habt ihr ein Gläschen Bier, so tuts mir einschenken; oder ein Gläschen Branntewein, das möcht mir auch wohl dienlich sein. Amen.

My request is to be considered: if you have a little glass of beer, then pour me one; or a little glass of brandy — that too would serve me well. Amen.

### Variations of the Wedding Song

The Hochzeitsbitter brought variety into the wedding song by sometimes reciting, in place of verses 8–23 of it, the hymn by Paul Gerhardt "Voller Wunder, voller Kunst" (Full of Wonder, Full of Art), from the 2nd through the 10th verse. This beautiful hymn is unfortunately much shortened in our provincial hymnal. Whoever wishes to come to know it in full must look it up in the Bollhagen (the Bollhagen hymnal); there it stands under No. 1096. Once the Hochzeitsbitter had made his round of all those to be invited and recited his little verses, his throat had likely become dry, and the ending of his song becomes quite understandable to us — but his task was not yet finished. He had to return to the wedding house and give an account of how he had attended to his office, and whether he had delivered all the invitations in proper order.

### The Hochzeitsbitter's Song of Accounting

This he does in the following song:

**Stanza 1:**

> Einleitung wie im Hochzeitsliede V. 1. Doch hab ich gebeten und geladen alle guten Freunde und Nachbarn. Sie lassen euch grüßen und stellen sich ein und wünschen Braut und Bräutigam viel Glück zur Hochzeit und Ehrentagen.

Introduction as in the wedding song, V. 1. Yet I have invited and bidden all good friends and neighbors. They send you their greetings and will present themselves, and wish bride and bridegroom much luck for the wedding and days of honor.

**Stanza 2:**

> Ferner der Hauswirt und dessen Hausgenossen werden so gütig sein, mein Kompliment noch ein wenig anzuhören. Also will ich euch sagen, wie ich die Hochzeitsgäste hab geladen zur Hochzeit und zu Ehrentagen, daß sie sich mögen am zukünftigen Freitag in eure Behausung verfügen und erscheinen und ziehen mit Braut und Bräutigam über Feld nach der christlichen Kirche vor die Trau und helfen den Ehestand zieren und vermehren; und nach der Vertrauung wieder ins bestimmte Hochzeitshaus zurück einkehren und vorlieb nehmen an Essen und Trinken, was der liebe Gott durch seinen milden und reichen Segen bescheren wird.

Further, the Hauswirt and his household will be so kind as to hear my compliment a little longer. So I will tell you how I invited the wedding guests to the wedding and to the days of honor: that they may on the coming Friday betake themselves to your dwelling and appear, and travel with bride and bridegroom across the field to the Christian church before the marriage rite, and help adorn and increase the married estate; and after the marriage, turn back again into the appointed wedding house and make do with the food and drink that dear God, through His mild and abundant blessing, shall provide.

**Stanza 3:**

> Auch habe ich gebeten die Manns und die Gesellen, die Jungfern und die Jungfrauen. Auch habe ich gebeten, daß sie mögen keine notwendigen Sachen vorwenden, damit die Tage der Ehe nicht geschwächet sondern vielmehr gestärket werden. Wenn sie würden wieder einen Sohn oder eine Tochter ausgeben oder eine andere Elation anstellen, sofern als ihr dazu geladen würdet, wollet ihr auch wiederum Hilf und Beistand tun.

I have also bidden the men and the young fellows, the maidens and the married women. I have also bidden that they plead no pressing matters as an excuse, so that the days of the marriage may not be weakened but rather strengthened. And should they in turn give away a son or a daughter, or arrange some other Elation (feast), insofar as you were invited to it, you too will again render help and assistance.

**Stanza 4:**

> Ich wünsche euch allen einen guten Abend, liebe Braut und Bräutigam, auch eurem Vater und Mutter, Brüder und Schwester. Lebet wohl, ihr vielgeliebten Brautleute mein; nun freue dich, lieber Bräutigam mein, daß deine liebe Braut wird kommen.  
> Indem wirst du sie rufen und schreien an: „Seid mir willkommen vor die andern all. „Zu euch steht mein Verlangen. „Ich will dich herzlich lieben und umfangen."  
> Der Ehstand hier auf Erden Soll billig geromentieret (?) werden.

I wish you all a good evening, dear bride and bridegroom, and your father and mother, brothers and sister too. Fare well, you my dearly beloved betrothed pair; now rejoice, my dear bridegroom, that your dear bride will come.  
And so you will call and cry out to her: "Be welcome to me before all the others. To you my longing is set. I will love and embrace you from the heart."  
The married estate here on earth shall rightly be geromentieret (?) — meaning uncertain; the transcriber's own question mark, probably a corrupt word, perhaps "honored" or "celebrated."

**Stanza 5:**

> Denn wofern wird sein ein Mann mit seinem Weibelein, da will denn auch Christus sein, aus Wasser machen roten Wein.  
> So gedenket daran, ihr Braut und Bräutigam fein, daß nach diesem Winter der Sommer wird brechen herein. Du bist von einem guten Stamm und von dem Erzvater Abraham; lieb Gott und freue dich, Bräutigam.  
> Putze die Pferde und spanne bald an; fahre mit Freuden über das Feld und hole die Braut ins Freudengezelt mit vielen Leuten und Gottes Segen; den rufet an auf all euren Wegen; so wird er euch trösten in der Not und euch segnen und geben Brot.

For wherever there shall be a man with his little wife, there too will Christ be, to make red wine out of water.  
So bear this in mind, you fine bride and bridegroom: that after this winter the summer will break in. You are of a good stock and of the patriarch Abraham; love God and rejoice, bridegroom.  
Groom the horses and harness up soon; drive with joy across the field and fetch the bride into the tent of joy with many people and God's blessing; call upon Him on all your ways; so will He comfort you in need and bless you and give you bread.

**Stanza 6:**

> So bitte, mein Bräutigam, und sei guter Dinge. Frühmorgens, wenn aufgeht die Sonne, so bringt sie dir Freud und Wonne.

So I ask, my bridegroom, and be of good cheer. Early in the morning, when the sun comes up, it brings you joy and delight.

**Stanza 7:**

> Also, meine geliebten Brautleute schon, was soll ich euch wünschen von meiner Person? Diesen Wunsch will ich euch schenken und verehren beiderseits dem Bräutigam und der Braut, die in Gott nun sind vertraut.  
> Der Ehstand, den ihr jetzt anfangt, ist ehrenwert, hat ihn doch der Herr Christus selber geehrt. Als er auf der Hochzeit zu Kana in Galiläa geladen ward, kam er mit seinen Jüngern beiderseits dem Bräutigam und der Braut zu Ehren.

And so, my already beloved betrothed pair, what shall I wish you from my own person? This wish I will give and bestow upon you both, on bridegroom and bride, who are now joined in God.  
The married estate that you now begin is honorable, for the Lord Christ Himself honored it, when He was invited to the wedding at Cana in Galilee and came with His disciples, in honor of both the bridegroom and the bride.

### Practical Instructions for the Wedding Preparations

**Stanza 8:**

> Auch bitte ich die Köchin, daß sie mag die Kost gut gar machen und wohl salzen, damit sie nicht nüchtern riecht, daß die Gäste nicht mögen loch (?) beißen noch sich die Zähne ausreißen, denn ich werde einen Knochen schmeißen. Wer weiß, wie wird's sacken (d. h. fördern), danach sich ein jeder zu richten. Ich werde auch nicht sein einer von den Schlechtesten.

I also ask the cook that she may cook the food well done and salt it well, so that it does not smell flat, and so that the guests need not bite into a hole (loch — ?) nor tear out their teeth; for I shall fling a bone. Who knows how it will sacken (that is, "prosper," go forward), by which each may take his measure. And I will not be one of the worst, either.

**Stanza 9:**

> Auch bitte ich die Auswaschers (Abwäscherin), daß sie mag die Schüsseln und Schemel und das Trinkgeschirr und Tisch und Banken wohl rein machen, auf daß Braut und Bräutigam und alle geladenen Hochzeitsgäste Lust und Liebe daraus zu essen und zu trinken haben.

I also ask the Auswaschers (Abwäscherin — the dishwasher-woman) that she may make the bowls and stools and the drinking-vessels and table and benches good and clean, so that bride and bridegroom and all the invited wedding guests may have pleasure and delight to eat and drink from them.

**Stanza 10:**

> Auch bitte ich den Holzhacker, daß er mag das Holz gut kurz hacken, auch fein trocken machen, daß das Feuer gut brennt, und die Kost sich nicht an den Kessel setzet.

I also ask the woodcutter that he may chop the wood good and short, and also make it nice and dry, so that the fire burns well and the food does not stick to the kettle.

**Stanza 11:**

> Auch bitte ich den Bierzapfer, daß er sich nicht möge die Nase vollsaufen und das Bier nicht in den Keller lassen laufen.

I also ask the beer-tapper that he may not drink his nose full and let the beer run off into the cellar.

**Stanza 12:**

> Auch bitte ich den Einschenker, daß er nicht zu voll schenke und übergießt, das dem Wirt nicht übel verdrießt.

I also ask the pourer that he not pour too full and spill it over, which would vex the host badly.

**Stanza 13:**

> Auch bitte ich die Herren Spielleute, daß sie nicht sehen auf den Beutel und nicht aufs Geld, auf daß der Geiger gut streichet fürs Geld, und der Pfeifer hält sich lustig wie ein tapferer Held.

I also ask the gentlemen musicians that they not look to the purse and not to the money — so that the fiddler bows well for the money, and the piper keeps himself merry like a valiant hero.

> Spielet mir Hochzeitsbote auch einen guten Abendrei (d. h. — reigen), mein Geld ist auch kein Blei. Hier sind noch 6 Dukaten; wer sie hat, dem sind sie wohl geraten.

Play me too, wedding-messenger, a good Abendrei (that is, Abendreigen — evening round-dance); my money is no lead either. Here are 6 more ducats; whoever gets them, they are well bestowed on him.

**Stanza 14:**

> Nun höret, was ich euch will wünschen für meine Person. Ich will euch nun wünschen dies und das. Gott wolle euch geben eine zufriedene Ehe, die Gott der Vater treulich beschert; der beschere euch Kindeskind, die im Alter eure Freude sind.

Now hear what I will wish you for my own person. I will now wish you this and that. May God grant you a contented marriage, which God the Father faithfully bestows; may He bestow on you children's children, who in old age are your joy.

**Stanza 15:**

> Gott der Sohn Jesus Christus, der gebe euch solchen Frieden und Eintracht, daß einer den andern gern lieben und trösten mag. Solches soll eine Braut anzeigen (erzeigen) ihrem Bräutigam, ein Bräutigam seiner Braut, sie sind reich oder arm, jung oder alt, lieblich oder schön, krank oder gesund, daß einer dem andern nicht bald feind wird, sondern von Tag zu Tag, von Woch zu Woch, von Jahr zu Jahr, in Glück oder Unglück, in Lieb oder Leid soll einer den andern lieben und trösten; je länger der liebe Gott euch im Ehstand läßt leben, je größer die Liebe und Freundschaft soll werden. Amen.

God the Son, Jesus Christ, grant you such peace and concord that one may gladly love and comfort the other. Such a thing a bride shall show to her bridegroom, a bridegroom to his bride, be they rich or poor, young or old, lovely or fair, sick or healthy — that one shall not soon become an enemy to the other, but from day to day, from week to week, from year to year, in fortune or misfortune, in love or in sorrow, one shall love and comfort the other; the longer dear God lets you live in the married estate, the greater shall the love and friendship become. Amen.

### Further Wedding Customs

Among the well-to-do, the wedding celebration was held both in the bride's house and in the bridegroom's house. If the bridegroom brought the bride into his paternal home, then the beginning of the wedding was at the bride's. But if he moved into the bride's farm, then she — especially if she were a widow fetching herself a Wehrsmann\*) — had to come to the bridegroom, and the wedding took place there.

\*) That is, a husband who managed the farm and held it in trust until the stepchildren came of age, for which reason he was also called an Aufhalter (a "holder" or caretaker).

### The Wedding Ceremony in the Church

The marriage took place in the church in the prescribed form. For it the bride arrived, with her Gespielen (her girl companions, playmates), on a great four-horse wagon, on the front of which sat the musicians with "Piepsack und Violen" (bagpipe and fiddles). The bridegroom came riding with his whole company of relatives and all the farmhands in the village, on the best horses, for which they had gleaming bridles. Guns were frequently fired on this occasion. Sharpened ordinances gradually abolished this shooting, since accidents sometimes occurred with it. The accompanying wagons carrying the female wedding company tried, driving at top speed, to win the foremost place from one another. Whoever arrived first at the church, by general opinion, would carry the person who would next be married.

### The Bride's Attire

The bride appeared in the customary black clothing, which among the well-to-do was finer, indeed sometimes of silk. She had a black, often silk, apron, and over it a woolen, Nesseltuch (a plain, usually unbleached cotton muslin — despite the literal "nettle-cloth," not made of nettle fiber), or linen apron tied on. Around her waist she wore the leather bridal girdle, densely set with silver, gilded bosses and closed at the front with a silver chain. The bodice (Schnürleib) too was laced with such a chain.

On her head she wore a crown called the "Flitter-Peik" (the bridal crown, hung with spangles). It was almost as tall as the grenadier caps of the 1st Guards Regiment. The lowest rim was a silver hoop, gilded and a hand's breadth high. Above it rose bows that met together at the top. All around hung a great quantity of silver Flitter (spangles), partly round, partly triangular. All these pieces — some of them very valuable — belonged to the family inventory and were used by every member of the family as occasion arose. Those of lesser means kept a crown of Knittergold (crinkle-foil, a cheap crumpled gold-leaf), set with many white and yellow spangles, in the form in which the crowns for corpses used to look\*).

The "Flitter-Peik" was fastened on the head with silver ribbon, and also with a silk ribbon that ran around the head toward the braid.

Around her neck the bride wore a white, blue-starched collar, which in some districts was worn somewhat upright against the head, in others hanging more onto the shoulder. To this was added a cloak of black cloth reaching to the calf, trimmed with black velvet and full of folds. It was worn about the shoulders. It was called a "Hocken." In place of a cloak-collar, a Rüsch-Brettchen (a stiff little ruff-board) was fastened at the neck, covered with black velvet and set with braid.

The rings too were inventory pieces, of silver and heavily gilded, so large that they could well be put over the thumb. In the middle was set an ornament with a border of precious stones.

### The Course of the Marriage Rite

The bride did not enter the church until after the wedding song had begun. Before her went the unmarried female persons; the married women followed her. On her entry she made, with all the female persons, an Opfergang (offering procession) around the altar.

The bridegroom, who had appeared in the church with all the male guests already before the bride, sat on the one side of the altar, the bride opposite him on the other. Immediately after the marriage he made the offering procession with the male persons. Then he took the young wife and led her out of the church to the bridal wagon, whereupon the whole procession hurried home again at the swiftest pace.

\*) These corpse-crowns stood everywhere in the churches of our region, but in the course of time have mostly been done away with. They were true dust-catchers. In Fritzow they were removed in 1895 during a renovation.

Having arrived at the wedding house, the party found it locked. It was the Hochzeitsbitter's task to gain admittance. And so the following dialogue arose between him and the Hauswirt, who stood before the door:

**Hochzeitsbitter:** Good evening, Herr Hauswirt, how is this meant? I have invited the guests into the house and not before the Heck (the transcriber glosses "(Hostor)," i.e. the *Hoftor*, the farm gate), to visit you most kindly and boldly.

**Hauswirt:** Where, then, have you been so long?

**Hb.:** We have been at the hunt, and we brought it so far to completion. And on the hunt we caught a pair of deer, of which one got away from us. Now we have come upon the trail and the notion that the deer has gone in here. If this be so, then answer me with a Yes!

**Hw.:** Yes.

**Hb.:** First, we would have in this wedding house free coming-in and going-out. Can we have that?

**Hw.:** Yes.

**Hb.:** Second, we demand a good host, a laid table, and a free cup-bearer with serving-glasses and serving-jugs and, as you find useful, whatever belongs thereto. Can that be so too?

**Hw.:** Yes.

**Hb.:** Third, we demand from the host, after the meal, light again on the table and on the dance-floor (Flor), so that we can see to play and to dance. Can we have that too?

**Hw.:** Yes.

**Hb.:** Fourth, when it then goes on toward midnight, the host must give again a free round of drinks, two tuns of beer, a Stiege (a score) of loaves of bread. Can we have that too?

**Hw.:** Yes.

**Hb.:** Fifth, we would have a good portion of an ox or a cow and a kettle full of Gruben (Graupen — pearl barley) with it, and 30–40 roasted sheep's heads besides. Can we have that too?

**Hw.:** Yes.

**Hb.:** Sixth, we demand from the Hauswirt 20–30 pounds of tobacco and 80–90 eherne Pfeifen with it. Can we have that too? *(eherne* literally means "of bronze/brass/iron"; given 80–90 pipes handed out at a village wedding, cheap disposable clay smoking-pipes are likely meant, but the literal reading is metal — retained as uncertain.)

**Hw.:** Yes.

**Hochzeitsbitter:** Now then, open the door and let us in, that we may be merry guests. Amen.

Upon this, bread and beer were carried out from the house to meet the wedding company. The bride first had to bite off a piece from the bread, then the bridegroom, and so all the guests in turn. But they did not swallow the morsel down; instead they saved it up.

Now the bridegroom went with the male guests into the parlor to the table; for the bride and her company, the meal was laid out on the Flur (the hall / entrance passage). Before her, as before the bridegroom, stood a wooden candlestick with 3 arms. The 3 candles were neither to be trimmed nor extinguished until they went out of their own accord. Any candle-ends that might remain were, like that morsel of bread, likewise carefully saved up — perhaps for superstitious purposes.

Because of the numerous assembly, which was fed for up to 8 days, the catering was costly enough. Ordinarily, among people of means, one needed 18 Scheffel (bushels) of bread-grain and 18 Scheffel of malt. For this were slaughtered 1 ox, 2 cows, 2–4 pigs, 6 or more sheep, besides uncounted poultry. The fish, which were consumed in great quantities, were mostly cooked beforehand and served cold.

### The Dishes Were:

1. Sweet groats — that is, barley groats with milk, or else millet or rice with milk and much butter on top, together with raisins. Over the top was strewn crumbled Pfefferkuchen (spice cake) or red sugar.
2. Fish, either cold or from the salt, with which horseradish cooked in buttermilk used to be served.
3. Beef with onions.
4. Mutton with white cabbage, turnips, and potatoes.
5. Pork, cooked black with dried fruit (Schwarzsauer).
6. And finally roast meat, depending on the season: roast pork (in winter), roast mutton (around Easter time, a recollection of the paschal lamb), roast goose (in autumn).

From the roast, the bridegroom sent his bride a piece by way of the Hochzeitsbitter, which used to be presented with the following little verse:

I would have a most friendly greeting for the much honorable and virtuous maiden bride. The honorable and virtuous bridegroom, through my mouth, sends her a thousand-thousand greetings in this hour. He sends the dish here; she will accept it quite kindly from me, since I am sent out with this dish from the bridegroom's hand. I am a poor, humble messenger, since I bring along a cabbage-stalk. The goats have indeed gnawed at it; I know well what pleases you. The bridegroom sends the maiden bride a piece of bread too; on it she shall feed herself up right well until death. The bridegroom sends her a roast as well, in which there are no bones. He sends her too a piece of the goose-breast. I hope the maiden bride has already had all this (*gemußt*, lit. "had to [have]"; the reading is doubtful — the intended sense is probably that she has by now received/been served it all). I kept going around peeking, thinking to attend to the maiden bride. As I came nearer, I saw that it must be she at this wedding. And so I kept stepping nearer to her; I had no horse, or else I would have ridden. I left my horse standing at home, since I came on foot. So may she be so good as to give me a little glass of beer or brandy — that too would serve me well.

For the aftercourse, "Göscht-Stuten" (that is, yeast-bread) with butter was passed around. Most people also began the feast with a slice of buttered bread, with which brandy was often offered to the male persons. The beer-jug meanwhile went diligently around. Each time a man set it to his lips, he gave his neighbor his hand, drank, and passed him the jug, which was taken up with the words "God bless it" (segne Gott) and passed on in the same manner.

After the meal, the dancing began. The musicians sat on a high chest in the Flur, where the dancing-floor also was. The married men, who no longer used to dance, smoked tobacco in the parlor and drank diligently along with it.

The first dance was always the "lange Streich" (a named dance; the sense of *Streich* here is uncertain — likely a long, sweeping chain or processional dance, as the description of couples filing under the bride's raised cloth suggests). The Brautdiener (the bride's attendant — this was the Hochzeitsbitter) led the bride by taking hold of the white cloth she held in her hand. The rest of the girls took hold of one another, two by two, by the hand. The Brautdiener and the bride had to take care not to be surrounded by them, or else a kind of forfeit had to be paid.

This dance was kept up for a good while, and indeed with many bows, since the pairs of girls often had to pass under the raised cloth of the bride.

### The Presenting (Zuführung)

Once the dance was finally concluded, the Hochzeitsbitter led the bride to the bridegroom waiting in the parlor, and likewise led a female dancer to each of the groomsmen (Trauführer), with the words:

I have thought of you and brought you a smart young girl. Should you scorn (verschmähest du) my hand, you will not scorn hers.

Another presenting-verse ran:

Good evening, dear bachelor, I have thought of you. I have brought you a pretty maiden bride. Her you shall lead to the game and the dance, here and there, up and down. And if she pleases you no longer, then do not send her back to me, nor to any other, either. Amen.

This maiden was each man's foremost dancing-partner during the festival. Every time, he had to open the dance with her before he was allowed to take another.

When, toward morning, everyone was weary from the dancing, the bridegroom fetched a horse from the stable and rode onto the Flur, where the bride came to meet him, in the one hand her three-armed candlestick, in the other a jug of beer.

The bridegroom drank, dismounted, danced the Brauttanz (bridal dance) with the bride, and then led her back to her place, and himself rejoined his company as well.

Soon afterward, then, came the Hochzeitsbitter to the last part of his many-sided activity on the wedding day — he led the bride to meet the bridegroom with the following words:

Good evening, most dear bridegroom.  
To you I come stepping.  
I had no horse, or else I would come riding.  
I left my horse standing at home,  
so now I must go on foot.  
I have become a colonel before evening  
and have gained a great multitude of people.  
Now the night is already at the door.  
Now I would gladly have quarters for my people.  
Now I ask the fine bridegroom  
whether he would not be so good as to take this maiden bride to himself into his quarters.  
So answer with a single word, yes or no.

**Bridegroom:** Yes!

Then take her to your side  
and live with her in joy and unity.  
Now I wish you a long life, peace  
and unity, and thereafter eternal blessedness. Amen.

On the morning after the marriage, the farmhands went out with all the inhabitants of the wedding house to fetch the so-called "Brauthahn" (the bridal cockerel). Armed with music and beer-cans, they all set off for the neighboring farm.

There they danced in the farmyard, the whole company was entertained, and they collected fruit, nuts, and the like into a sack that the Hochzeitsbitter carried.

Together with the neighbor folk, they went on to the next farm, where the same thing took place. So the ever-growing procession wandered from house to house, and would even "punish" a neighboring village if it lay nearby, until it arrived back again at the wedding house.

Here the now quite full sack was cut open across the middle, everyone snatched as much of the contents as he could grab, whereupon all of it was devoured amid cheering and laughter.

The young wife still sat that day in her bridal finery. In the afternoon she moved into her house. On the day after that, she put on the Haube (married woman's cap) and bonnet, drove to church alone with the married women, made her offering as a young wife, and, after the singing of a hymn, was blessed as such by the pastor.

Beforehand, she had to give her attendants a large cheese, and so did the young man. This cheese they divided among themselves. The pastor too received a cheese.

When the guests took their leave, they were given a piece of roast, a bottle of beer, and a "Bärmstuten" (a yeast-loaf) to take along — as it were in thanks for the gifts of milk, butter, eggs, and so forth, which they, especially the relatives, had brought along as a contribution to the entertainment.

That was a wedding 150 years ago. Let us wish that the good folk did not spoil their stomachs with all that eating and drinking.

Admittedly, the danger, I believe, lay further off with the simple foods of that time than it does today, when the tables are set with so many fine dishes. At any rate, it was a "fine discipline" that the whole celebration proceeded in fixed orders. Each and every thing had its firmly appointed place; and merry, for all that, one still was.

Not everything hangs on the outward orders, to be sure, but a good deal does; and one should beware of abolishing, without necessity, such orders as one has received from the fathers.
