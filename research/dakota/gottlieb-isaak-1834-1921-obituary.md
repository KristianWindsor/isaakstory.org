# Gottlieb Isaak Obituary

Gottlieb Isaak was born on the 24th day of October, 1834, in the colony of Kulm, Bessarabia, South Russia. On December 17, 1854, he was married to Louise Schultz, which union was blessed with twelve children, seven boys and five girls. Two boys died in Russia and two sons and two daughters died in America. In the spring of 1878 he emigrated to America with his family where they settled on a farm in Hutchinson county, in the settlement known as Kulm. In the spring of 1885 the homestead in Hutchinson county had been pretty well taken up, so he moved to Hebron, N. D. After a few months there he removed to Mercer county in the same state, where all the boys took up homesteads. In moving from South Dakota to North Dakota, the livestock and farm implements were shipped by train by way of Minneapolis, while the family with their household goods made the long weary journey overland in ox wagons. Conditions were primitive on the plains of North Dakota, where they were compelled to sleep under their wagon boxes until they had temporary sod houses built. Even at that time they were threatened several times by revolting Indians, but fortunately never forced to battle. Sixty miles was their nearest railroad station, New Salem, N. D., whither they hauled their grain to market with ox-teams. In Mercer county he served in various capacities as county official. After six years on his farm in Mercer county, together with his wife and two youngest children, he moved to Eureka, S. D., where he entered business in partnership with his son, David.

On December 17, 1904, he celebrated his golden wedding at which a large part of the populace of Eureka took part, being the first occasion of that kind in that town. May 1st, 1905, his wife died; and from that time he has stayed with his children at Eureka and other places, the last six years making his home with his daughter, Mrs. Magdalena Sailer, at Tripp. His strength was failing him gradually and he has had occasional attacks of rheumatism. In the last few months his physical weakness became more pronounced and on January 12th, a stroke of apoplexy forced him to bed, which also caused the loss of his speech. He was conscious until a few hours before his death, which occurred at 10:55 p. m., January 20. The deceased was 86 years, two months and twenty-seven days old. Six children survive him, three sons, Daniel, Salomon and Emanuel, and three daughters, Mrs. John Klein, Mrs. Magdalena Sailer and Mrs. Rev. O. Bruntsch. The number of grandchildren was 73, of which 54 are living, and 45 great-grandchildren are living.

Funeral services were held last Sunday at the Iowa Lutheran church, conducted by Rev. Taeuber. The remains were taken to Eureka for burial.
