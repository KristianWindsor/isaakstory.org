David Isaak was a respected son of Gottlieb Isaak and his wife Louise, nee Schulz. He was born at Kulm, South Russia, April 17, 1860. Here he was christened and later confirmed in the Lutheran faith. In the year 1877 he came to America with his parents, grandparents and brothers and sisters. The family settled at first at Scotland in this state and entered upon agricultural pursuits. On the 30th. of August, 1883 David Isaak was married to Miss Hanna Hetzler. This bond was blessed with the births of five children, two of whom preceded the father in death.

Three years after their marriage, Mr. and Mrs. David Isaak removed with several relatives to Mercer County in the, at that time, Territory of Dakota, where again they entered upon agricultural occupations. Even at that time the deceased did not enjoy the best of health and at times complained that farming was too strenuous for him. For this reason he removed his family to Eureka and founded the business which he, together with his sons, managed until his death.

At the time Mr. Isaak came to Eureka, there was no Lutheran congregation here. He was one of the founders of the Eureka Lutheran church and served her faithfully until his death. David Isaak was naturally of a quiet and retiring disposition but despite his lack of forwardness was at all times a true and devoted servant of the church. He attended services regularly and was unfailing in his attendance at Lord's supper.

About five years ago, Mr. Isaak suffered a severe illness from which he never wholly recovered. He was thereafter subject to heart trouble and his physician foresaw that he was to meet with sudden death.

This prophecy proved true for on the morning of Friday, December 21, he was suddenly called from his earthly home, and death found him in midst of duty.

David Isaak is survived by his widow and three children, namely Josephine and Edward, at home, and Albert, who is enlisted in the military service of the government. He is also survived by his old father, Gottlieb Isaak, and four brothers, namely: Jacob, Daniel, Solomon and Immanuel, and also three sisters: Mrs. Regina Klein, Mrs. Magdalena Sailer and Mrs. Antonio Bruntsch.

(From the life of David Isaak, read by Rev. L. Seehase at the funeral services Sunday, Dec. 23.)
